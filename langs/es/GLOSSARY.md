# Spanish glossary (Latin American dub canon)

The terms are in Spanish, because that is what they are. The notes are in English
so that anyone reviewing the pack can follow the reasoning.

> The Japanese source terms have been removed from this document: the public
> repository does not distribute the game's text. Extract your own with
> `mrb extract` and you will see the original next to every translation under
> `work/`.

The canon is the Latin American dub (Etcétera Group / Fox Kids, 2002), checked
against Doblaje Wiki, Medateca and the Spanish Wikipedia.

## The referee's ritual lines (frozen, do not improvise)

| Spanish line | Note |
|---|---|
| **¡Que así sea!** | CANON opening: «¡Que así sea! Oficialmente declaro esta pelea una robobatalla de sumisión...». If the line acts as a question before the confirm button, use `¿Están de acuerdo?` and keep `¡Que así sea!` as the referee's answer afterwards. |
| **¡De acuerdo!** | The confirm button before a robobatalla. Once pressed, the referee replies with the canon formula above. |
| **¡A robobatallar!** | The core shout of the opening: «¿Listos, medaguerreros? ¡Medabots... a robobatallar!». |
| **¡Función cesada!** | The referee's ruling ("Function Ceased!"). Not confirmed verbatim in the dub, but it is the coherent fan reconstruction and it sounds like a ruling. In descriptive text: `ha dejado de funcionar`. |

## Style rules

1. **Register.** Neutral es-419, second person singular (`tú`); never `vosotros` or
   `vos`. UI instructions use one formula throughout: the imperative (`Elige...`).
2. **es-419 vocabulary, mandatory.** `costo` (not `coste`), `mazo` (not `baraja`),
   `robar cartas` (not `sacar`), `sobre` (not `paquete` or `pack`), `réferi` (not
   `árbitro`). Banned Spain terms: `Medaluchador`, `Banda Roborobo`, `Medapiezas`.
3. **Brand pattern.** Prefix `Meda-` plus a Spanish word. Proper names and brand
   technicalities stay untranslated: Medabot, Metabee, Rokusho, Tinpet, KBT/KWG,
   Drive.
4. **Space is tight.** This is a TCG UI on a handheld: always pick the shortest
   variant that does not lose precision. Approved abbreviations: `Brazo der./izq.`,
   `Sin ataque`, `pieza(s)` instead of `Medaparte(s)` in chips and dense rules text.
   One abbreviation per term, applied uniformly.
5. **Collisions everyone must respect.** The support *type* is `Apoyo`, the support
   *role* is `Soporte`, and the cheer effect is `Ánimo`; the charge *stat* is
   `Carga`, the keyword is `Impulso`, and the movement one is `Propulsión`;
   `Curación` is not `Reparación`.
6. **Phases** use the Spanish TCG convention: `Fase de robo`, `Fase de preparación`,
   `Fase de acción`, `Fase final`. The status stays `En Espera` even though the
   phase is called `de preparación`.
7. **Capitals.** Keywords, statuses and rule names take an initial capital
   (`Quemadura`, `Guardia`, `Impulso`); lowercase when they act as an ordinary verb
   or noun in rules text. The game's full-width brackets become `[ ]`, as in
   `[Disparo]`.
8. **Markers from the original stay untouched.** Placeholders `{0}` / `{1}`, line
   breaks `\n` and tags such as `<color=#FF3174>` or `<size=-5>` are copied exactly.
   Game numbers (2000, 3000, 10000) and the parametric `X` (`Barrera: X`) are kept
   as they are.
9. **Padding.** Japanese strings padded to a fixed width with ideographic spaces are
   translated without the padding.
10. **Original bugs get reported, not imitated and not silently fixed.** Three
    entries in the buff table are broken in the game itself: one carries the wrong
    text, two have no text at all. Still pending in-game QA: `Furia de Reloj`,
    `Restonación`, `Eito`/`Eight`, and the exact wording of `¡Función cesada!`.
